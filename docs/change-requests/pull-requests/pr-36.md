## Robo & Chromia — the 20 Oct round, re-cut onto today's main

Five changes: three on the Robo reports and downloads, two on the shared Attendance register. Nothing outside Robo, Chromia and the shared Attendance module is touched.

These were written on 18 Oct's main and could not be applied after the 20 Oct code review rewrote the Attendance register (shared form and table styles, "View as" read-only, an action-log trail inside every write's transaction). They are re-done against that module, not forced onto it — the trails, the per-row failures and the read races it introduced are all kept.

**One schema change**, additive, already on Neon from 20 Oct: `attendance_operator."sortOrder"`, recorded as `scripts/0137-attendance-roster-order.sql`. It is `IF NOT EXISTS`, so it is the record of what was done and what a fresh database runs. Nothing is dropped or renamed.

### 1. Downloads → Reference Sheet: a design is the same design however the spaces fall
The design search ignored capitalisation but only *collapsed* runs of space. The register does not disagree about how many spaces there are — it disagrees about whether there is one. "Taj vein" setups run to **D-1470** (last slab 17 Oct); "Tajvein" setups stop at **D-1398** (31 Jul), so an operator who typed it without the space was handed a reference sheet for a batch three months old. Likewise "CRYSTALLO - ARLINA" returned "No previous production record found" while its slabs sat under "CRYSTALLO-ARLINA". Spacing is now dropped entirely. Checked against the whole register first: of 45 distinct names across 104 setups, this merges exactly those two pairs and nothing else.

### 2. Reports → Production Rate per Hour: an hour's delays are the hour's, cut at the hour
The tooltip read the register correctly and added it up wrongly — D-1473's 05:00 hour on 19 Oct listed 1 hour 9 minutes of a sixty-minute hour. Two faults, both in how the hour was formed:

- **A delay was filed whole under the hour it began in.** It is now cut at each boundary and listed in every hour it ran through, keeping its code (D1 03:55 → 04:12 is 5 minutes of the 03:00 hour and 12 of the 04:00 one). The register holds 29 delays longer than an hour, the longest being T1 22:00 → 06:00, 480 minutes — a power cut whose seven covered hours, all with no slabs *for that reason*, read "No delays".
- **The total was the lines added up.** The floor logs a delay against the slab it held up, so one stoppage is written down once per slab and the rows overlap. Total delays is now the minutes of the hour that were under a delay, counted once, which cannot exceed 60; every row is still listed, and the box says when they overlap.

Audited across the whole register: of 628 hours carrying a delay, **40 claimed more than sixty minutes** (worst 480). After: **zero**, worst exactly 60, and **51 further hours** that had a delay running through them were reading "No delays".

### 3. Reports → Production Rate per Hour: each shift's slab count
The band under the hours already named each stretch of the line. It now also says what that shift produced — SHIFT B over 44 SLABS — being the block's own hours added up, so the figure is the plotted line read off rather than a second answer. Each slab is counted once, under the shift whose window its production time fell in, and a day's three shifts add up to the day. Two lines rather than one because it was measured: the sentence on one line is 166 px against 52 px per hour, so it would need a four-hour block, and runs routinely end in two-hour ones.

The band grew by one line and the chart grew with it — in `chart-tokens`, where `HOURLY_PLOT_H` is also what the Reports page reserves for its loading placeholder, so the placeholder grows with the band instead of leaving a jump when the chart loads.

### 4. Attendance: an In-Time may be ten minutes old, and the Out-Time is stamped, not typed
An In-Time may be no earlier than ten minutes before the plant clock and not in the future; at 18:43 the earliest is 18:33. The field opens on the clock for a row that has none yet, shows "18:33 or later" underneath, and turns red with the reason — and Save with it — the moment anything earlier is typed. The Out-Time is never typed: it is stamped with the clock, so it cannot be back-dated or post-dated. The box is a div and two buttons, not an input.

Both are enforced on the SERVER against its own reading of the plant clock (Asia/Kolkata), not the tablet's. Comparisons go the short way round the clock, so the night shift marking in across midnight does not meet a wall; a tablet up to two minutes fast is believed, or ordinary drift would refuse every honest entry. An unchanged In-Time carried back when a row is saved for its Line is not re-judged, or a row entered this morning could never be corrected.

### 5. Attendance: the roster is the owner's twelve, in the owner's order
The register sorted alphabetically. The floor's list is not: it is the order the line is called in — Jagadeesh, Rajesh, Saravanan .L, Kiran, Praveen S., Venkatraj, U Kumar, Aaquib, Sebul, Shiva, Mani Bharathi, Yogender Reddy. Position is now data (`sortOrder`) and the only thing any reader orders by: the screen, the + Add Operator panel, the Downloads picker and the Excel sheet. A new operator is appended, read inside the add's own transaction so two tablets adding at once cannot take the same number; a rename may write the name and the active flag only, so it cannot reorder. The alphabetical comparator is deleted rather than left unused. `prisma/seed-attendance.ts` holds the twelve and numbers them 1..12 (`npm run db:seed:attendance`, idempotent).

`tests/roboShiftAttendance0123` held the attendance schema to `scripts/0123` column for column, so no column could ever be added to those tables again. It now reads the later scripts too, named one at a time, so the model is still exactly what the scripts built and a new attendance column is a line in that test as well as a script.
