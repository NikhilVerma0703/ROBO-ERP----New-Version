## Robo & Chromia — the round of 20–26 Oct 2026

Nine changes in one branch: five on the shared Attendance register, three on Robo Reports and Downloads, and role-based access control for both line modules. Nothing outside Robo, Chromia and the shared Attendance module is touched.

**There ARE schema changes this round** — three, all additive, **and none of them has been applied to Neon yet.** Run these three, in order, before the deploy:

`attendance_operator.sortOrder`, `attendance_leave.leaveType`, and the `PLANT_INCHARGE` value on the `Role` enum. Each is idempotent, so a second run is harmless. Nothing is dropped or renamed.

### 1. Attendance: an In-Time may be ten minutes old, and the Out-Time is stamped
An In-Time may be no earlier than ten minutes before the plant clock and not in the future; at 18:43 the earliest is 18:33. The Out-Time is never typed — it is stamped with the clock, so it cannot be back-dated or post-dated. Both are enforced on the SERVER against its own reading of the plant clock (Asia/Kolkata), not the tablet's; the page runs the same two functions so the operator is told at once. An unchanged In-Time carried back when a row is saved for its Line is not re-judged, or a row entered this morning could never be corrected.

### 2. Attendance: the roster is the owner's twelve, in the owner's order
The register sorted alphabetically. The floor's list is not alphabetical — it is the order the line is called in: Jagadeesh, Rajesh, Saravanan .L, Kiran, Praveen S., Venkatraj, U Kumar, Aaquib, Sebul, Shiva, Mani Bharathi, Yogender Reddy. Position is now data (`sortOrder`) and the only thing any reader orders by: the screen, the + Add Operator panel, the Downloads picker and the Excel sheet. A new operator is appended, so adding one never moves another; a rename may write the name and the active flag only, so it cannot reorder. The list is shared by both ERPs, because the table is.

### 3. Reports → Production Rate per Hour: each shift's slab count
The band under the hours already named each stretch of the line. It now also says what that shift produced — SHIFT B over 39 SLABS — being the block's own hours added up, so the figure is the plotted line read off rather than a second answer. Each slab is counted once, under the shift whose window its production time fell in, and a day's three shifts add up to the day. Two lines rather than one because it was measured: the sentence on one line is 166 px against 52 px per hour, so it would need a four-hour block, and runs routinely end in two-hour ones.

### 4. Attendance: Leave Taken, and whether the operator told anyone first
"Request Leave" is now "Leave Taken", and carries a **Leave Type** — Informed Leave (dark green) or Uninformed Leave (dark red) — immediately after Operator Name on the form, in the register, in the Downloads results table and in the Leave sheet. The colours are `#1a7a42` and `#cf2f26`, not plain dark red/green: that pair is ΔE 2.0 under protanopia — the same colour to about one man in twelve — while these are at 8.5. The words are always printed alongside, so nothing depends on colour. The type is required and refused on the server; the code is stored, never the words, and "UNINFORMED" is parsed before "INFORMED", which it contains.

### 5. Downloads → Reference Sheet: a design is the same design however the spaces fall
The design search ignored capitalisation but only *collapsed* runs of space. The register does not disagree about how many spaces there are — it disagrees about whether there is one. "Taj vein" setups run to **D-1470** (last slab 17 Oct); "Tajvein" setups stop at **D-1398** (31 Jul), so an operator who typed it without the space was handed a reference sheet for a batch three months old. Likewise "CRYSTALLO - ARLINA" returned "No previous production record found" while its slabs sat under "CRYSTALLO-ARLINA". Spacing is now dropped entirely. Checked against the whole register first: of 45 distinct names across 104 setups, this merges exactly those two pairs and nothing else.

### 6. Reports → Production Rate per Hour: an hour's delays are the hour's, cut at the hour
The tooltip read the register correctly and added it up wrongly — D-1473's 05:00 hour on 19 Oct listed 1 hour 9 minutes of a sixty-minute hour. Two faults, both in how the hour was formed:

- **A delay was filed whole under the hour it began in.** It is now cut at each boundary and listed in every hour it ran through, keeping its code (D1 03:55 → 04:12 is 5 minutes of the 03:00 hour and 12 of the 04:00 one). The register holds 29 delays longer than an hour, the longest being T1 22:00 → 06:00, 480 minutes — a power cut whose seven covered hours, all with no slabs *for that reason*, read "No delays".
- **The total was the lines added up.** The floor logs a delay against the slab it held up, so one stoppage is written down once per slab and the rows overlap. Total delays is now the minutes of the hour that were under a delay, counted once, which cannot exceed 60; every row is still listed, and the box says when they overlap.

Audited across the whole register: of 628 hours carrying a delay, **40 claimed more than sixty minutes** (worst 480). After: **zero**, worst exactly 60, and **51 further hours** that had a delay running through them were reading "No delays". No mock data and no hardcoded batch anywhere in it.

### 7. Attendance: the plant's day, the night shift, and a register with no holes
Five changes to Daily Attendance:

- **It opens on today, and today is the plant's** (Asia/Kolkata) — a tablet on another timezone no longer opens the wrong day.
- **A night shift is one row and says so.** In 22:00 → out 06:00 is eight hours; the Out now carries the date it belongs to instead of printing a bare "06:00".
- **Yesterday has to be finished first.** A new entry is refused for an operator whose previous day is unfinished, with "Previous day's attendance entry is incomplete. Please complete it first." and the date to go to, enforced on the server. Exempt: an operator on recorded leave that day, and one who has never been marked in before it — without the second, the rule would have refused the whole plant on day one.
- **"Invalid entry/time."** Every refusal opens with those words and then says what would work (at 04:56 the earliest is 04:46). The ten-minute window governs TODAY's row; an earlier day is a correction and its times are typed, or a day whose In-Time was never recorded could never be filled in. Nothing is loosened — every time on a day that has ended is in the past — and a future date is refused outright.
- **The day's register is emailed at 07:00 and 19:00 IST** to gibin@thepacific.group (`ATTENDANCE_REPORT_EMAILS` overrides). Every operator including the blanks, with who is in, who is out and who is on leave and of which kind. Scheduled at 01:30 and 13:30 UTC, but the route reads the plant clock to decide which report it is. It goes through the run-once guard the other report crons have used since the plant's Telegram group got the hourly post twice (`lib/cronSend.ts`, scripts/0133), keyed `attendance:<day>:<slot>` — so a double fire, a retry after a timeout and two instances racing a cold start all end with one email, and a send that fails releases the key for the next run. One report, not one per ERP: Robo and Chromia share the tables. `CRON_SECRET` like every other cron here, and the path is in `CRON_ROUTES` — without which middleware would 302 it to /login and Vercel would log the redirect as a success.

### 8. Role-based access: the tablet on the line marks today, the in-charge keeps the register
Two logins stand in front of the same register and they are not the same person. The one on the line is a shared tablet nobody signs out of; the one in the office keeps the register. Until now there was only the first, and it could do everything.

**The operator** (`robo.operator@`, `chromia.operator@` — role Robo Operator / Chromia Operator), through either ERP's door:

- **opens today and no other date.** That one rule is all four of the asks — no previous dates, no future dates, no editing a previous record, no filling in a missed one, and no way round the incomplete-previous-day hold, which clears when the Plant In-Charge finishes the day that is holding it;
- **writes a row once.** The morning's In-Time, the evening's Out-Time stamped onto the same row — adding is not amending, which is why it is asked column by column. A written row offers one button ("Mark out now"), not an editor; a finished one offers nothing;
- **never sees Leave Taken or the register's Downloads.** Not drawn, not fetched, refused by the route, and refused at the edge before that;
- **never sees Download Slab Records** on Robo → Downloads. Reference Sheet and QC Rejection Analysis are still theirs, and so is the Reports page;
- **reads the roster** and does not add to, rename or remove from it.

**The Plant In-Charge** is a new role (scripts/0139), created in Users & Roles on the Shop Floor like every other login: both plant modules, every attendance feature, both slab exports. Not an administrator — no /office, no /sales, no Users & Roles, and none of the three destructive Chromia controls that were made admin-only on CHR-4, which nothing in this instruction asked to reopen.

**No password is anywhere in this branch.** The three logins are made in Users & Roles and their passwords are bcrypt'd into `users.passwordHash`, exactly as the ERP has always done it; a role is what the code knows. There is a test that checks the literal is absent, written as a pattern so the test does not carry it either.

**Three layers, because hiding a menu item is not enough.** `src/lib/plantAccess.ts` is the one table — two tiers, six rights — read by the edge (middleware + routeCaps), by the sidebar, by the route gates and by both screens. A path refused by every method is refused at the edge as well, through **both** doors: a list naming only the Robo spelling would be a Chromia tablet with the run of the register.

> **One thing to know before merging.** `ROBO` and `CHROMIA` are the operator tier, which is what their labels have said since they were made. Any login holding one of them — `chromia@` and `chromia.manager@` among them — marks attendance and no longer keeps the register. Giving one of those the **Plant In-Charge** role in Users & Roles is the whole of putting that back.

### 9. Reports → the batch's Design Name, beside the Batch Number
Typing a Batch Number now shows that batch's Design Name to the right of the field, updating as it is typed and reading "— no batch on record" when there is none. It comes from the real setup→design mapping already behind the batch picker, folded by the same rule change 5 uses, so a batch whose setups spell the design two ways shows one name. Display only: no report calculation changes, and no batch number is special-cased anywhere.
